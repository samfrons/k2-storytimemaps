The Four Storms · 1939 – 2008

Scrub the century

Each step below is one disaster, placed on the real terrain of K2. The ✝ pins mark where the mountain kept them — every date and toll from the documented record, every position approximate.

1939 · July – August

A Mountain to Die On

Wiessner turns back 800 feet from the top; the camps beneath him are stripped by mistake. Dudley Wolfe waits alone at Camp VII, and three Sherpas — Pasang Kikuli, Pasang Kitar, Phinsoo — climb into the clouds to bring him down. None return.

4 lost · the highest rescue attempt then ever made

1986 · June 21 – August 10

The Black Summer

Twenty-seven summits by five lines — and thirteen dead, across the whole mountain: an avalanche, two descents that never ended, a crevasse at Base Camp, the fixed ropes, and a storm that sealed seven climbers into Camp IV at 8,000 metres.

13 lost · the summer K2's arithmetic changed

1995 · August 13 – 15

One Day as a Tiger

Six climbers — Alison Hargreaves first among them — summit at 6:45 in the evening into a perfect sky, and a 160 km/h storm kills them all on the descent. Jeff Lakes, who turned back, dies of exposure two days later.

8 lost that season · and one climber tried by the press

2008 · August 1 – 2

The Bottleneck

Eighteen summit — some at eight in the evening. At 20:30 the serac above the Bottleneck calves, cutting every fixed rope. Eleven die in the couloir Wiessner refused in 1939: the deadliest single accident in the mountain's history.

11 lost · the most documented, the most contested

The Wall

Where the Mountain Keeps Them

Every documented loss of the four disasters, placed by the altitude at which the mountain took them. In 1939 and 1986 K2 killed on every floor of the climb — a crevasse at its foot, an avalanche on the approach, the ropes, the Shoulder. In 1995 and 2008 nearly every mark sits in one thin band: the death zone, above 8,000 metres, where summits come late and storms shut the door.

Different decades, different instruments, the same few thousand metres of ice. The 1939 men waited fifty years for the truth to come down; the 2008 dead were tracked by satellite and are argued over still. The mountain has never cared how well we record what it does.
